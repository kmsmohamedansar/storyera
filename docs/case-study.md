# Case study: YAKUZA, The Borrowed Life

**Spoilers for Volumes One–Three.** By Mohamed Ansar Kader Meera Sahib. Copyright © 2026, all rights reserved; this page and the excerpts are excluded from MIT.

The original conversation audited the prologue and 53 chapters of the supplied draft. That was a README-guided workflow demonstration before the installable skill existed. The examples below were selected afterward; they are not blind test results. The full manuscript remains outside this repository. Original line numbers refer to the supplied snapshot, not a future edition. Exact excerpts and its SHA-256 are recorded in [provenance.json](../examples/yakuza/provenance.json).

## 1. Two groups become one

![Two separate groups: 34 shipped and 14 newly rescued](assets/yakuza-counts.png)

| Established evidence | Source |
| --- | --- |
| “There were thirty-four names.” | [Chapter 44, original line 10729](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#cohort-ledger) |
| Tanabe describes that group as gone and shipped. | [Chapter 44, original line 10793](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#cohort-shipped) |
| The blue container is described as the next shipment. | [Chapter 44](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#cohort-next) |
| “Fourteen children. Between five and eleven.” | [Chapter 47, original line 12232](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#cohort-rescue) |
| “Fourteen of them were home now.” / “Twenty were still out there.” | [Chapter 53](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#cohort-ending) and [remaining count](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#cohort-missing) |

**Classification:** contradiction, high confidence in the supplied reading. The ending subtracts the new rescue group from the previously shipped cohort without establishing overlap. As established, 48 children are accounted for: 14 rescued and 34 still missing.

**Minimal proposal:** leave 34 missing at the ending. **Alternative author decision:** explicitly make the rescued fourteen members of the original ledger and revise the earlier account of shipment status. That alternative needs a narrative bridge, not just a different sum.

## 2. The first job happens twice

Chapter 17 establishes paid receipt-checking work, a spreadsheet discovery, and a ¥42,000 debt payment. Chapter 25 introduces “Your first job,” explains what a spreadsheet is, and says “He had never been paid as *Asa*.” See [earlier work](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#work-earlier), [spreadsheet](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#work-grid), [payment](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#work-payment), [later job](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#work-first), and [pay reset](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#work-pay-reset).

**Classification:** contradiction, high confidence. A first formal office posting would be compatible with prior work, but the pay and spreadsheet resets still require reconciliation.

**Minimal proposal:** make Chapter 25 his first office posting, distinguish earlier receipt checks from new computer skills, and replace the first-pay claim. Keep the printer comedy and the meaningful moment of receiving his payslip.

## 3. A deadline contradicts itself inside one reply

Nakamura says the redemption period had not ended, then says Asa returned “two days *after* the date.” The surrounding account also puts the return a week after pawning. See [return](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#pawn-return), [question](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#pawn-question), and [reply](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#pawn-reply).

**Classification:** contradiction, high confidence for the before/after wording; the intended commercial arrangement needs author input.

**Repair choices:** Asa was late but Nakamura would have accommodated him, or Nakamura sold before the deadline. Choose deliberately: the two versions assign different responsibility to the pawnbroker. No real-world pawn-law conclusion is asserted.

## 4. Growth is not a contradiction

Arthur's arms shake under the practice sword early on. June 2027 supplies fourteen months of training before describing his stronger body. These facts support an **explained change**, not a strength inconsistency. The precise calendar elsewhere can still need repair without erasing the training arc. See [weakness](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#body-weak) and [training](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#body-training).

## 5. An age progression needs a birthday, not an automatic error

Yui is five in the early 2026 material, six by June 2027, and seven by December 2027. A birthday in the second half of the year can reconcile all three. This is **compatible chronology**; ask for a birthday only if a dated scene makes it necessary. See [five](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#age-five), [six](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#age-six), and [seven](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#age-seven).

## 6. Preserve the premise's uncertainty

The bodily-habits passage explains language and procedural memories surviving in Asa's body: “A word he’d never learnt came easily. His thumb found the right place on a phone without being told.” See [Chapter 19](../examples/yakuza/excerpts.md#language). This is an explicit explanation within the speculative premise.

Arthur's belief that Asa is gone does not independently prove the metaphysics of the transfer. Whether Asa can return remains an author-controlled mystery or open question. The audit should not invent an answer.

## What this demonstrates

Evidence can make a finding concrete and a repair small. Equally useful is refusing a tempting false positive. The case study demonstrates the approach on selected passages; it does not establish exhaustive recall, universal accuracy, or independent expert agreement. The saved [curated JSON report](../examples/yakuza/curated-report.json) audits only the excerpt file, not the full novel. The full original audit is not reconstructed here as if it were a fresh packaged-skill run.
